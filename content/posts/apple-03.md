Most iPhone photos are wonderful straight out of the camera, and that is precisely the problem for anyone who wants to edit them. The processing that makes a snapshot look good (local tone mapping, aggressive noise reduction, sharpening) is baked into the HEIC file before you ever see it. Push those files in an editor and they fall apart quickly.

**ProRAW** changes the deal. It still uses Apple's computational pipeline, so you keep the multi-frame merging that gives iPhone shots their clean shadows, but it saves the result as a 12-bit linear DNG *before* the final look is applied. You get the detail, and you get to decide what to do with it.

## When to bother

ProRAW files are large (often 25 MB or more at full resolution) and they need editing to look their best. Use it when:

- The scene has a **huge dynamic range**: sunsets, interiors with bright windows, backlit portraits.
- You plan to **print or crop heavily**. The 48 MP sensor on recent Pro models only gives you its full resolution in ProRAW or the dedicated 48 MP HEIC mode.
- You want **your own colour**. Apple's rendering is pleasant but recognisable; if you have a look you like, RAW is where you apply it.

Leave it off for casual snaps, bursts, and anything you will only ever view on the phone.

## Turn it on

*Settings → Camera → Formats → Apple ProRAW* (or *Pro Default* on newer versions). Then in the camera, tap the **RAW** badge to toggle it per shot. You can choose between 12 MP and full-resolution capture; 12 MP is plenty unless you know you need the pixels.

## Exposing for RAW

The habit that improves RAW shots more than any other: **protect the highlights.** Tap to focus, then slide the exposure down a touch until the brightest part of the frame stops clipping. Shadows in a ProRAW file lift beautifully; blown skies are gone forever.

A few more habits:

1. Use the **2x or 24 mm** crop rather than digital zoom beyond it; those are real sensor crops.
2. Turn off **Night mode** when you can brace the phone. The long-exposure merge is impressive, but it smears fine detail.
3. Keep the **lens clean**. Nothing ruins a RAW file like a fingerprint haze.

## A phone-only editing flow

You do not need Lightroom on a desktop to get good results. In the Photos app:

1. **Exposure and Brilliance** first. Brilliance is the closest thing to a highlight/shadow recovery slider.
2. **Warmth and Tint** to fix white balance, which is the main thing Apple's auto rendering sometimes gets wrong indoors.
3. **Sharpness to zero, then back up slightly.** ProRAW files come with less sharpening than HEIC, so a little is fine; a lot looks brittle.
4. Export as HEIC or JPEG for sharing, and keep the DNG.

If you want more control, the Lightroom mobile app opens DNGs directly and syncs edits, and its lens profiles for iPhone are excellent.

## The honest limits

ProRAW does not turn a phone into a full-frame camera. Small sensors still produce noise at high ISO, and the wide lens still shows distortion at the edges. What it does is remove the ceiling that HEIC puts on editing. For a device that is always in your pocket, that is a lot.
