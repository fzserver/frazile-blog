Every Pro iPhone year is a camera year, but this one is different in kind rather than degree. The iPhone 18 Pro and 18 Pro Max have a mechanical aperture in the main camera: six laser-cut blades that step between four openings. It is the feature that made me stop the keynote and rewind. Here is the whole phone, camera first.

![Depth of field control on the iPhone 18 Pro main camera](/media/2026/09/e58799af15fee46c.jpg)
*Image: Apple*

## The variable aperture

The 48MP Fusion main camera now opens to ƒ/1.48 for low light and closes to ƒ/1.8, ƒ/2.8 or ƒ/4 when you want everyone in a group photo sharp. Until now every iPhone had one fixed aperture and simulated the rest in software: Portrait mode's blur is a depth map, and the phone cannot make a background *sharper* than the lens delivers. Now it can. Apple pairs the blades with a new sensor and a new computational pipeline, and says the result is sharper, more detailed shots throughout the range.

The Camera app grows a **Pro controls** panel with aperture, shutter speed, white balance and a live histogram. There is an API too, so Halide and friends get the blades on day one. Photographic Styles gain texture and grain sliders, which suggests Apple has noticed how many people run their photos through a film-emulation app afterwards.

![Low-light shot at ƒ/1.48](/media/2026/09/f11717f92030ad7b.jpg)
*Image: Apple*

Video gets cinematic effects that can be applied after the fact on 60 fps clips, 4K Dolby Vision time-lapse, and an Audio Mix that can now isolate music as well as voices.

## Apple Reference Image

The quieter camera announcement may matter more in a few years. **Apple Reference Image** is an opt-in feature that signs the sensor data at capture and stores an unalterable reference through Private Cloud Compute, a digital negative that can prove a photo is what came out of the camera. Alongside it, Apple is adopting the SynthID standard for marking AI-generated and edited images. For a phone that will also ship with Image Playground generating photorealistic pictures, having both halves of that story on the same device is the right call.

## A20 Pro and the vapor chamber

The A20 Pro is Apple's first 2 nm phone chip: a 6-core CPU with neural accelerators in each core, a 7-core GPU that Apple rates 40 percent faster than the A19 Pro, and a dual 16-core Neural Engine with twice the AI throughput. Memory bandwidth is up 50 percent. The packaging changes are the interesting part: the die sits beside the memory instead of under it, taking the RAM out of the heat path, and the vapor chamber has three times the surface area of last year's. Apple claims up to 40 percent better sustained performance, which is the number that matters for games and for the local models Siri AI now runs.

![The A20 Pro chip](/media/2026/09/22c1ca59ffc19a38.jpg)
*Image: Apple*

Two more Apple chips replace parts that used to come from elsewhere. The **N1** handles Wi-Fi 7, Bluetooth 6 and Thread. The **C2** modem is the second generation of Apple's own cellular silicon, now with mmWave in the US, faster uploads, and 15 percent less power per byte.

## Battery and charging

- iPhone 18 Pro: up to 36 hours of video playback.
- iPhone 18 Pro Max: up to 45 hours, and Apple's new real-world model rates it at up to 30 hours of mixed use.
- 50 percent charge in about 15 minutes on a cable, 30 minutes wireless.
- Five minutes of wired charging on the Pro Max gives about seven hours of video.

The eSIM-only models, which now include India's neighbours in the Gulf, Japan, Canada, Mexico and the US, get two extra hours of video from the space the SIM tray used to take. India still gets a physical SIM.

## Design and display

The Dynamic Island is smaller and shows up to three Live Activities while keeping Face ID. Four finishes: black, silver, glacier and a new burgundy that is going to be the popular one. Storage runs 256 GB, 512 GB, 1 TB and 2 TB. Cases come in TechWoven, silicone and clear, plus a new $29 Wrist Strap alongside the Crossbody Strap from last year.

## Who should buy it

If you shoot a lot with your phone, this is the biggest single-year camera change since the 48MP sensor arrived, and the aperture is something no software update can retrofit. If you are on a 17 Pro and do not care about photography, the A20 Pro and the battery gains are real but not visible in daily use; wait for the spring iPhone 18 if you want a cheaper way in.
