Twelve years after GTA V, the date on the box has finally stopped moving. Grand Theft Auto VI is out on **Thursday, November 19, 2026**, and Take-Two has spent the last two earnings calls saying so in as many ways as it can. This post collects what is actually confirmed: when, where, how much, what it runs like, and what the game is.

## The date, and how we got here

The first trailer landed in December 2023 with a "2025" at the end of it. In May 2025 Rockstar moved the game to May 26, 2026, and in November 2025 it moved again, to November 19. Since then the message has been consistent: Take-Two's August 7 earnings report described its fiscal-year outlook as reflecting a "high degree of confidence" in the date, and Rockstar North has said the same in public. Pre-orders opening in June and a full marketing cycle running since the summer are the stronger signal. Publishers do not take money for a date they expect to miss.

## Platforms, price and editions

- **Platforms:** PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. There is **no PC version announced**, which matches Rockstar's history: GTA V reached PC 19 months after consoles, Red Dead Redemption 2 a year after.
- **Standard Edition:** $79.99. That puts it at the top of current console pricing, and it will be quoted in every pricing argument that follows.
- **Ultimate Edition:** $99.99, with additional in-game content.
- **Pre-orders** opened June 25 and include the *Vintage Vice City* pack.
- **Pre-load:** digital copies can be downloaded from **November 12**, a week before launch. Rockstar has not published a final install size yet, so clear plenty of space early.

## The Extended Look

On August 27 Rockstar did something new for a game reveal: it premiered a 27-minute *Extended Look* on Netflix, then put it on YouTube six hours later. It is the longest look at the game so far, with free-roam driving through Vice City, missions that go wrong in the usual Rockstar way, cutscenes and a long stretch of Lucia and Jason working together.

Rockstar says the footage was captured on a **base PS5**, not a PS5 Pro, and it looked it in both directions. The density of the city and the lighting are a clear step past anything else in the genre, and the frame rate is visibly 30.

## 30 fps, for now

Content creators who visited Rockstar North came back with the same answer. GTA VI currently runs at **30 fps on every console**, the PS5 Pro included, and Rockstar has "nothing to share" on a performance mode. Head of development Rob Nelson reportedly said the team would still talk to its technical staff about a 60 fps option at launch or later.

Read that as "30 fps on day one, maybe more later". A 40 fps mode on 120 Hz displays is the realistic hope for the Pro, since it is a much smaller jump than 60. If frame rate matters more to you than being there on day one, waiting for the first patch notes costs nothing.

## Leonida, Vice City and the two leads

The game is set in **Leonida**, Rockstar's version of Florida. **Vice City**, its Miami, is the centre, and around it are the Leonida Keys, the Grassrivers wetlands (the Everglades), Port Gellhorn, Ambrosia and Mount Kalaga National Park. Rockstar has described it as its biggest and most varied map.

The story follows **Lucia Caminos** and **Jason Duval**, a couple pulled into a criminal conspiracy after a job goes wrong. It is a Bonnie-and-Clyde setup, and Lucia is the series' first female lead you cannot skip. The trailers lean heavily on social media, body-cam footage and viral clips, and that looks like the satire's main target this time, the way advertising and celebrity were in GTA V.

## What about GTA Online?

Rockstar has presented GTA VI as a single-player game, and it has not detailed an online mode or said how GTA Online on current consoles will carry over. Given how much of Take-Two's revenue GTA Online has made for a decade, something is coming. It just is not part of the launch pitch.

## What to do before November 19

Pre-order only if you want the pre-load. There is no chance of it selling out digitally, and the Standard Edition is the one to buy unless the Ultimate content is specifically what you want. Free up the storage, update your console, and if you are on the fence between a PS5 and a PS5 Pro purely for this game, wait for launch-day technical breakdowns before you spend the difference.
