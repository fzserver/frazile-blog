Marvel's Wolverine has been out for a little over a week, and it has two stories that do not match. Critics are lukewarm, and it is Insomniac's lowest-scoring Marvel game by a distance. Players are buying it anyway, faster than any PlayStation exclusive since Spider-Man 2. Both are worth understanding before you buy it.

## What it is

Wolverine is a **single-player, story-driven action game** for **PS5 only**, from Insomniac, the team behind Marvel's Spider-Man. It is set three years after Logan walks away from Team X. He is pulled back in to investigate a string of mutant abductions run by the billionaire **Bolivar Trask** and his **Reavers** militia. The plot is built around Logan's broken memory and what he did during the years he cannot remember. **Liam McIntyre** plays Logan, and Marcus Smith and Mike Daly directed.

It is **not open world**. That is a deliberate break from Spider-Man: you move through a series of large, handcrafted locations, and the whole design is built around close-quarters combat, with claws, takedowns and a healing factor that turns defence into something you manage rather than a health bar you protect.

## What the reviews say

It sits around **76–77 on Metacritic** from more than 120 critic reviews, with about **two-thirds of critics recommending** it on OpenCritic. For comparison, Spider-Man 2 scored 90.

The praise is consistent. The combat is slick and satisfying in the first hours, the animation work on Logan is superb, and the character writing is the best part of the game. McIntyre's Logan is tired and funny, and the quieter scenes land.

So are the complaints:

- **Combat that wears thin.** The first ten hours of dismembering Reavers feel great. By the end, many reviewers found that fights were repeating rather than developing.
- **Too many quick-time events**, especially in the set pieces, where the game often takes control away just as it gets exciting.
- **Linear levels with weak platforming** between the fights. Players coming from swinging through Manhattan feel the difference most.

The consensus is a well-made, well-acted game that plays it too safe. Nobody is calling it bad; they are calling it an Insomniac game that is only good.

## The sales number, and what it means

Analytics firm **Alinea Analytics estimates** Wolverine sold about **1.9 million copies in its first three days**, worth about **$130 million**. About a million of those were **digital pre-orders**, bought before a single review went live. It also debuted at number one in the UK physical charts, and buyers rate it 4.8 out of 5 on the PlayStation Store.

Two caveats. First, this is a third-party estimate. **Sony has not announced a figure**, and the company tends to wait for a round number. Second, the pre-order share shows how strong the Wolverine brand is: a large part of that launch was always going to happen whatever the reviews said. The number to watch is the next two months, when word of mouth matters more than the name.

Even with both caveats, this is a very strong launch. In three days it passed the estimated lifetime sales of Death Stranding 2.

## Who should buy it

- **You love the character.** This is the best-realised Logan in a game, and that alone is worth it for a lot of people.
- **You want a focused story game** with no map full of icons. Its linearity is a feature if you are tired of open worlds.
- **Wait for a sale** if you mainly want Spider-Man's freedom of movement, or if repetitive combat is what normally makes you drop a game. With GTA VI arriving in November, Wolverine will not stay at full price for long.
